# Axe’n’Stax Brand Guidelines

**Direction:** Copperline / Path to the Peaks  
**Brand idea:** *An open voxel world that belongs to the people who play and build it.*

The identity should feel adventurous, welcoming, practical and player-owned. It sits on the cusp between wilderness and simple technology: mountains, forests, mines, copper wiring, lantern light, wind and water power. It should **not** read as steampunk, crypto, futuristic sci-fi, or a Minecraft clone.

---

## 1. Core brand principles

1. **World first.** Axe’n’Stax is a voxel world and game platform before it is a technology story.
2. **Freedom without jargon.** Open source, moddability, self-hosting and player ownership are benefits; avoid leading with blockchain/crypto terminology.
3. **Progress feels physical.** Building, wiring, mining, moving, powering and exploring should feel tangible rather than abstract.
4. **Friendly, not childish.** The brand must work for younger players and adults who grew up with voxel/sandbox games.
5. **Frontier, not primitive.** The visual world combines nature and basic engineering. Electricity exists, but it is practical copper-wire technology rather than neon futurism.

---

## 2. Logo system

### Primary symbol
The core symbol combines:
- **Mountain peaks** — exploration, scale, possibility.
- **Copper path** — a journey through the world; also a subtle nod to practical electrical infrastructure.
- **Lit mine entrance** — discovery, making and the transition from wilderness into player-built spaces.

The mountain suggests an **A** and the winding copper path suggests an **S**. The mine stays a secondary discovery detail. The main wordmark keeps its A and S normal and legible; it does not turn letters into illustrations.

### Wordmark
`AXE’N’STAX` uses a custom heavy, square-shouldered sans design. The **N** may be copper as a restrained accent. Do not reshape the A into a mountain or the S into a path in the primary wordmark.

### Copper line
The copper line is a secondary brand device. It can sit beneath a wordmark or divide content, but should not appear on every asset.

### Recommended variants
- `svg/axenstax-stacked-dark.svg` — primary lockup on dark backgrounds.
- `svg/axenstax-stacked-light.svg` — primary lockup on light backgrounds.
- `svg/axenstax-horizontal-dark.svg` — mark + wordmark for navigation/header use.
- `svg/axenstax-horizontal-light.svg` — light-background equivalent.
- `svg/axenstax-wordmark-dark.svg` / `...light.svg` — wordmark only.
- `svg/axenstax-mark.svg` — detailed standalone mark.
- `svg/axenstax-mark-flat.svg` — preferred small/utility mark.
- `svg/axenstax-mark-mono.svg` — one-colour production use.
- `svg/axenstax-app-icon.svg` — full-square app artwork; export at each platform’s required raster size. This is not an Android adaptive foreground layer.
- `svg/axenstax-app-icon-rounded.svg` — rounded website/social tile.
- `svg/axenstax-block-icon.svg` — illustrative single-block emblem; use at 128 px or larger.
- `svg/axenstax-favicon.svg` — small browser/site icon.

### Clear space
Use at least **0.5× the height of the capital A** around the wordmark. Around the standalone mark, use clear space equal to roughly **12.5% of the mark width**.

### Minimum sizes
- Stacked lockup with subtitle: **400 px** wide digital / **80 mm** print.
- Horizontal lockup with subtitle: **600 px** wide digital; use wordmark-only below this.
- Wordmark alone: **140 px** digital / **30 mm** print.
- Flat mark: **48 px** digital minimum.
- Detailed mark with mine light: **96 px** minimum.
- At 16–32 px, use the favicon; fine details are intentionally removed.

### Do not
- Add gears, pipes or Victorian ornament to make the identity “steampunk”.
- Turn the logo into a Bitcoin/crypto symbol.
- Add crowns, shields or heraldic status motifs.
- Use the mountain/path concept inside multiple letters of the wordmark.
- Place the detailed mark over visually busy game screenshots without a quiet field or dark overlay.

| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Primary dark | Deep frontier | `#0D1B1E` | UI backgrounds, headers, dark logo fields |
| Neutral dark | Deep rock | `#2B2B2B` | Secondary backgrounds, outlines |
| Primary green | Forest green | `#2E6B43` | Landscape, buttons, supporting brand colour |
| Deep green | Pine | `#1F3F2E` | Marks, dark foliage, UI accents |
| Primary warm | Copper | `#D27B3E` | Path, power, links, selected states, restrained highlights |
| Warm highlight | Lantern | `#F4C16F` | Light sources, notifications, rare highlight |
| Primary light | Mountain stone | `#E6E0D1` | Main wordmark/light text, snow/stone |
| Environmental blue | Sky blue | `#3F7FBF` | World imagery and occasional informational states |
| Supporting earth | Earth | `#8B5E3C` | Terrain/material accents |

### Colour balance
For brand-led screens, aim roughly for:
- **55–70%** dark/neutral
- **15–25%** greens
- **5–10%** stone/off-white
- **3–8%** copper
- **<5%** lantern yellow

Copper should feel valuable because it is used sparingly.

## 4. Typography

### Display / brand headings
For the logo, use the supplied custom outlined wordmark, not retyped text. For headings, retain **Noto Sans Display Extra Condensed Black** or a similarly heavy sans as the supporting type choice. The logo subtitle uses outlined DejaVu Sans.

Characteristics:
- sturdy
- compact
- readable
- slightly engineered without feeling futuristic

Use uppercase for short display phrases and navigation labels.

### UI / body
Recommended: **Inter**.

Use sentence case for body copy. Keep line lengths moderate and avoid technical density on player-facing pages.

### Type treatment
- Headings: bold, short, direct.
- UI labels: concise and functional.
- Marketing copy: active verbs, plain English.
- Avoid pseudo-medieval copy and excessive survivalist language.

## 5. Voice and messaging

### Brand voice
**Adventurous. Practical. Open. Human. Slightly rebellious.**

The player should feel invited into a world of possibilities, not sold a technology stack.

### Lead with
- Explore
- Build
- Create
- Power
- Mod
- Share
- Host
- Belong
- Make it yours

### Avoid leading with
- Bitcoin
- blockchain
- crypto
- decentralised infrastructure
- Web3
- tokenomics

These can be explained where technically relevant, but are not the emotional proposition.

### Example messaging
**Primary proposition:**  
> A voxel world you can make your own.

**Supporting line:**  
> Explore, build, power and reshape open worlds — then take them further.

**Community / ownership:**  
> Worlds built by players.

**Developer/open-source:**  
> Mod it. Host it. Change the rules.

## 6. Visual world

### Environment
The default Axe’n’Stax visual world should suggest:
- alpine and forest landscapes
- rivers and waterfalls
- stone, timber, soil and copper
- mines, workshops and small settlements
- practical mine carts / rail
- simple electrical lighting
- copper wires and poles
- wind generation
- water wheels / hydro power

This is a **starting world**, not a restriction on what mods can become.

### Technology level
Think **early practical electrification in a frontier world**, not Victorian steampunk and not modern clean-tech.

Good visual cues:
- exposed copper wiring
- warm filament/lantern-like electric lights
- simple switches
- wood/stone structures with functional engineering added to them
- mine rails and powered carts
- windmills and water wheels integrated into settlements

Avoid:
- brass goggles and steampunk costumes
- decorative pressure gauges everywhere
- giant boilers as a default motif
- neon circuitry
- sleek futuristic machines

## 8. UI guidance

### Dark mode / game launcher
Preferred default:
- Background: Deep Frontier `#0D1B1E`
- Primary text: Mountain Stone `#E6E0D1`
- Primary CTA: Copper `#D27B3E`
- Secondary CTA: transparent with Stone outline
- Positive/world states: Forest Green
- Powered/active state: Copper or Lantern

### Light web pages
Use warm off-white rather than pure white. Deep Frontier or Pine for headings, Copper for actions and dividers.

### Buttons
Primary: copper fill + dark text.  
Secondary: dark/transparent + stone border.  
Do not use excessive gradients or glossy game-UI effects.

---

## 9. Photography / game artwork treatment

Brand artwork should show **people doing things** in the world: exploring, wiring, building, travelling, gathering or looking out over something they helped create.

Prefer:
- broad landscapes with a human-scale foreground character
- warm points of player-made light against natural environments
- visible signs of practical engineering embedded naturally in the world
- friendly characters rather than aggressive combat poses

Avoid making every image about mining, axes, survival or industry. The world should feel bigger than its name.

---

## 10. Brand architecture

### Axe’n’Stax player brand
Game-first. Adventure, creativity, open worlds, ownership and community.

### Technical/open-source communication
The same visual identity can become quieter and more functional. Discuss engine architecture, self-hosting, Signet, proof-of-play and Bitcoin only where they matter.

Bitcoin is an **implementation and economic choice**, not the visual identity of Axe’n’Stax.

## 11. File notes

The SVG pack is a clean vector reconstruction of the selected branding direction. It is designed to be editable and production-friendly rather than a literal auto-trace of the concept artwork.

All 19 SVGs contain real vector paths/shapes, with no embedded bitmap, external resource, script or font dependency. All wordmarks and subtitles are already outlined. The copper ribbon uses one native three-stop linear gradient; the flat and mono marks have no gradients. The detailed illustration uses supporting shades of the core palette for rock, foliage and mine materials.

Dark/light filenames describe the intended background. Logo backgrounds are transparent; app tiles and favicon carry their own dark field. The mono mark and UI icons use `currentColor` (black by default); when embedding inline, set CSS `color`. For external image files, set the SVG root `color` or replace `currentColor` with a chosen hex.

Use unique ID prefixes if inserting several gradient-bearing SVGs inline into one HTML document. Loading through `<img>` avoids shared-document gradient ID collisions.

This is a hand-built vector interpretation of the approved board, not an exact tracing of its rendered textures. The vector paths are the reusable identity assets; the concept landscape is not included.
