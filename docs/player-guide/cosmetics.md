# Skins & the Wardrobe

> **This guide is written for the web version** (play in Chrome/Edge). The same
> Wardrobe, painting, export and import work in the desktop app; the only real
> difference is where your skins are saved (see [Where your skins are saved](#where-your-skins-are-saved)).
> It doubles as the **test walkthrough** — there's a [Testing checklist](#testing-checklist) at the end.

Your character has a **skin** — the picture wrapped around your avatar. You can keep a
whole **wardrobe** of skins (like a wardrobe of outfits), **paint your own in the Workshop**
by blowing your avatar up with the Bellows and painting it just like a block, and switch
which one you're wearing whenever you like. Skins are **purely cosmetic** — they never change
how you move, how big you are, or how much damage you take.

---

## Open your Wardrobe ("Your look")

1. Be **in a world** (the web version drops you straight into one).
2. Press **`Esc`** to open the pause menu.
3. Click **"Your look"**.

You'll see the **Wardrobe**: a grid of little **3D characters**, one per skin you own. A
fresh player has one — the default look.

- The skin you're **wearing** has a **✓** after its name (and a blue tint).
- **Click any character** to **select** it (a blue outline appears). Selecting doesn't
  change what you're wearing — it just chooses which one the buttons below act on.

Under the grid are the actions for the **selected** skin, then the "make a new skin" row,
then **Back** (or `Esc`) to return to the game.

---

## Wear a different skin

1. Click a skin in the grid to select it.
2. Click **"Wear"**.

The **✓** moves to it, and your avatar changes immediately — you'll see it on both your
third-person body (press `F5` to look at yourself) and your own hand in first-person.

---

## Make a new skin (four ways)

All four add a **new** entry to your wardrobe — they never overwrite an existing skin.

### 1. New (blank) — paint from scratch
Click **"+ New (blank)"**. A new skin is created (starting from the plain default look so
it's never invisible) and you're taken into the **Workshop** to paint it — see
[Paint your skin in the Workshop](#paint-your-skin-in-the-workshop) below.

### 2. New from a colour — instant recolour
Under **"New from a colour:"**, click **Red / Green / Blue / Yellow / Pink**. This makes a
new skin tinted that colour **and wears it right away** — handy when you just want a quick
look without painting.

### 3. Upload a PNG — bring your own
Click **"⬆ Upload PNG"** and pick a **64×64 PNG** skin file from your computer. It's added
as a new skin and worn. The layout is the **standard 64×64 "classic" (Steve) format**, so
anything from a free online skin editor fits.

> If you pick an image that **isn't 64×64**, you'll get a friendly message and nothing
> changes — that's expected, not a bug.

### 4. Bring in a Minecraft skin — from a username
See [Bring in a Minecraft skin](#bring-in-a-minecraft-skin) below — type a Minecraft Java
username and pull that player's current public skin straight into your wardrobe.

## Paint your skin in the Workshop

You paint your avatar the **same way you reskin blocks**: blow it up with the Bellows and
paint it.

1. From the **lobby** (your world list), click **🔧 The Workshop** — a private creative room
   where you can **fly**.
2. A **mannequin of your avatar**, wearing your current skin, is standing on the floor.
   *(Arrived from "Your look → Edit"? You're dropped straight here with that skin on the mannequin.)*
3. Hold the **Bellows** (it's in your Workshop hotbar), aim at the mannequin, and **hold
   right-click** — it swells and **locks**, exactly like blowing up a block.
4. **Fly around it** and paint. Press **Tab** for the paint panel — the colour wheel, a
   **`#rrggbb` box** you can type a colour straight into, all 16 dye colours, your
   **recent colours**, the six tools and every option live in there, so you don't need
   dyes in your hotbar and there's nothing to memorise.

| Input | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Tab** | Open/close the **paint panel** — colours and every option. |
| **Left-click** | Paint the spot you're aiming at. |
| **Shift + left-click** | **Straight line** — rules a line from the last spot you painted to the one you just clicked (same side of the same part). |
| **B** | Cycle the **shading brushes**: Brush → **Lighten** → **Darken** → **Noise** → Brush. |
| **F** | **Fill** on/off — one click floods the whole side you're aiming at. |
| **R** | Move the limbs **apart** (or back together) so you can reach the insides of the arms and legs, the top of the head, and under the body. |
| **V** | **Clothes on/off** — on means you're painting the clothes layer, off means you're painting the body underneath. |
| **M** | Mirror — paint one side and the other side matches. |
| **L** | **Grid** on/off — draws the pixel grid on the mannequin, and a yellow box shows exactly where your click will land. |
| **G** | Eyedropper — grab a colour that's already on the skin. |
| **Z** / **X** | Undo / redo the last stroke. |
| **P** | **Pin** — saves the painted skin, wears it, and deflates the mannequin. |

> **If a patch seems impossible to click, press R.** The arms sit against the body and the
> legs against each other, so some faces are hidden until you move the limbs apart. This is
> the single most useful key in the painter.

> You can also paint by **holding a dye** and left-clicking, just like reskinning blocks —
> see [Dyes, Fibre & Magnesium](dyes-fibre-and-magnesium.md) for the 16 colours. You never
> have to, though: the panel has all of them plus a full colour wheel.

### The six tools

The **Tool** row in the panel picks what a click does. Only one is ever on.

| Tool | Key | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Brush** | **B** | Paints the colour you've chosen. With **nothing in your hand and no colour picked**, it rubs out instead — that's always been true and still is. |
| **Fill** | **F** | Floods the matching area on the side you're aiming at. Stops at a change of colour and never crosses onto another part. Needs a colour — you'll be told if you haven't picked one. |
| **Eraser** | — | Rubs out. On the clothes it leaves a see-through hole; on the body it puts the plain default skin back. |
| **Lighten** | **B** | Makes the pixel a bit paler — about a tenth of the way to white. |
| **Darken** | **B** | Deepens the shade — about a tenth of the way to black. |
| **Noise** | **B** | Wobbles the brightness slightly at random, so a flat colour looks like stone or cloth instead of paint. |

**Lighten, Darken and Noise step once per click.** Hold the button and sweep and each pixel
still only changes once — so a slow hand doesn't burn a white hole where a fast one made a
gentle highlight. Let go and click again for a second step.

**B** walks between Brush and the three shading brushes; from Fill or the Eraser it takes
you back to the Brush.

### Colours: wheel, hex, dyes and recent

- The **colour wheel** mixes anything you like.
- The **`#rrggbb` box** beside it takes a hex code typed or pasted in — the same notation
  every skin tutorial online uses. It only takes effect once all six digits are there, so
  it won't fight you halfway through typing.
- The **16 dye swatches** are the standard palette.
- **Recent** keeps the last eight colours you used — from the wheel, the hex box, a dye or
  the eyedropper — so a shade you mixed can be got back without mixing it again.

**Shift + click** rules a **straight line** from the last spot you painted, which is far
easier than trying to drag one freehand down a four-pixel-wide arm. It works with whichever
tool you're on (except Fill), and it stays inside the side you're painting.

### Clothes: the layer over the body

A skin has two layers — the **body**, and a slightly larger **clothes** layer over the top
for hats, hair, jackets and sleeves.

When you open a skin for painting, the clothes start **on** if anything is painted on them
and **off** if they're empty — so you always begin on the layer that has something to work
with. A brand-new skin opens on the body; a skin you brought in from Minecraft with a hoodie
opens on the clothes. Press **V** (or use the panel) to switch.

**Whatever you can see is what you're painting.** Turn the clothes off and they vanish and
your clicks land on the body; turn them on and they reappear and your clicks land on them.
You can never accidentally paint a layer that's hidden.

**Which skin am I painting?** The mannequin always shows the skin you're **wearing**, so
**Pin overwrites your worn skin**. To tweak a *different* one, either **Wear** it first, or
click **Edit ✎** on it in "Your look" (that drops you into the Workshop with that skin
loaded). Want to keep the old version too? **Duplicate** it in "Your look" before you paint.

## Classic or Slim arms

Minecraft has **two player shapes**, and so does AxeNStax:

- **Classic** ("Steve") — chunky **4-pixel-wide** arms. The default.
- **Slim** ("Alex") — **3-pixel-wide** arms. One pixel narrower, taken off the *outside* of
  each arm, and the whole arm hangs half a pixel lower — exactly as Minecraft does it.

It's the **same 64×64 picture** either way. Choosing Slim doesn't change your painting; it
changes how wide the arms are *drawn*, on your body, on your first-person hand, and on the
little 3D thumbnail in the wardrobe.

**Where the toggle is.** Two places, and they mean the same thing:

- In **"Your look"**, select a skin — the **Arms: Classic / Slim** row sits just under the
  Wear / Edit / Duplicate buttons. It saves straight away, and if that skin is the one you're
  wearing, your body changes shape on the spot.
- In the Workshop paint panel (**Tab**), the **Arms: classic / slim** row reshapes the
  blown-up mannequin **live** while you paint — the texel grid and the yellow "where your
  click lands" box follow it — and **Pin** saves your choice onto the skin along with the
  pixels.

**Imports know already.** When you bring in a skin from a Minecraft username, we read
Minecraft's own model flag: an Alex skin arrives set to **Slim** and says so
(*"Slim-arm skin — arms set to Slim"*). **Refresh** re-checks it too, so a player who
switched from Steve to Alex comes back with the right shape.

**Duplicate** keeps the setting; a **brand-new** or **uploaded** skin starts on Classic.

> **Painting tip:** a slim arm's front is only **3 texels** across, so the grid over that
> face shows 3 columns instead of 4. Switch Arms *before* you paint the arms if you can —
> switching afterwards doesn't lose any pixels, but the outermost column of a classic arm
> is simply not shown on a slim one.

## Export — wear it in real Minecraft

This is the headline trick: **paint a skin here, then wear it in actual Minecraft.**

From **"Your look"**, select a skin and click **"Export ⬇"** (next to Rename).

On the **web**, the skin downloads as a `.png` straight to your **Downloads** folder, named
`axenstax-<skin-name>.png` (your skin's name, lowercased, with anything that isn't a letter
or number turned into a hyphen — a blank name becomes `axenstax-my-skin.png`). A little help
panel then pops up — **"Now put it on in real Minecraft in 3 steps:"** — telling you to go to
**minecraft.net → Log in → Profile**, upload the PNG, and pick the **arm shape**. Step 3
names the one to pick: it reads the skin's own [Arms setting](#classic-or-slim-arms), so a
Classic skin says *choose Classic* and a Slim one says *choose Slim / Alex*. Match it and the
skin looks the same there as it does here. Click **Got it!** (or `Esc`) to dismiss it.

> On the **desktop app**, Export opens a Save dialog so you choose where the `.png` goes.

## Bring in a Minecraft skin

Already have a skin in real Minecraft? Pull it straight in.

1. In the wardrobe, click the dashed **"Bring in a Minecraft skin"** card.
2. In the **"Bring in a Minecraft skin"** box, type a **Minecraft Java** username and confirm.
3. A moment later that player's **current public skin** lands as a new wardrobe entry
   (tagged **"from Minecraft"**) and is worn.

It uses only **public** Minecraft data — **no Microsoft/Mojang login**, and you never type a
password. Older **64×32** "legacy" Minecraft skins are automatically expanded to the modern
**64×64** layout on the way in, so they work like any other skin. If something goes wrong
you'll get a friendly note and nothing else changes, e.g.:

- *"We couldn't find anyone by that name…"* — check spelling; usernames are **case-sensitive**
  and this is **Java Edition only** (not Xbox/Bedrock).
- *"Can't reach Minecraft right now… try again in a minute!"* — the lookup service had a moment.
- *"Slow down a bit!…"* — you've looked up a lot of skins quickly; wait about a minute. There's
  also a **server-side** limit behind this message, so it can't be worked around by retrying fast.
- A player who's still on the **default** Steve/Alex look (they've never set a custom skin)
  can't be imported — you'll get a friendly *"no custom skin to import"* message instead of a
  copy of the default look.
- A corrupted/unreadable image or an invalid username also gets its own friendly error —
  nothing else changes.

### Refresh — re-pull the latest
If a player (or you) changes their skin in real Minecraft later, open that imported entry and
use **Refresh** to pull the new version. It re-pulls by the stored Minecraft ID, so it keeps
working even if they rename.

> **Privacy:** the username is a **public** Minecraft handle. On the web it passes through
> **our own** game server straight to Minecraft. We never write it to disk or log it —
> it's held in a short in-memory cache (a few minutes, so repeat lookups don't hammer
> Mojang) and then discarded. On desktop your computer talks to Minecraft directly. The
> game works fine if Minecraft is unreachable — import is optional and never blocks play.

## Manage your wardrobe

With a skin **selected** in the grid:

- **Duplicate ⧉** — makes an independent copy ("… copy"). Edit the copy without touching the
  original — great for trying a variation.
- **Rename** — type a new name, then press **Enter** or click **✓**. (An empty name is
  ignored.)
- **Delete ✕** — removes the skin. Two safety rules:
  - You **can't delete the skin you're wearing** — the button is greyed out (and if you try,
    you'll see *"Wear another skin first"*). Wear a different one, then delete.
  - You **can't delete your last remaining skin**.

---

## Where your skins are saved

**On the web, your whole wardrobe is saved in your browser, on this device** (in the
browser's local storage). That means:

- It's there instantly and works **offline**.
- It **survives a page refresh** and closing/reopening the tab.
- It does **not** travel to other devices or other browsers (the web version keeps things
  local — no account, no cloud sync).
- **Clearing your browser data / site data wipes it.** Use a different browser profile and
  you'll start fresh.

> On the **desktop app**, the wardrobe is instead saved to a file on your computer, so it
> survives quitting and relaunching the game.

## Testing checklist

Run through these in the **web** build. Each should behave exactly as described — anything
that doesn't is worth noting.

**Create**
- [ ] **New (blank)** → you're taken into the Workshop with a plain default skin on the mannequin (not invisible).
- [ ] **New from a colour** (e.g. Blue) → a new blue skin appears in the grid **and is worn** (✓).
- [ ] **Upload PNG** with a real **64×64** skin → it's added and worn; your avatar shows it.
- [ ] **Upload PNG** with a **wrong-size** image → friendly error, **nothing else changes**.

**Paint in the Workshop**
- [ ] **🔧 The Workshop** (from the lobby) shows a **mannequin of your worn skin** standing on the floor.
- [ ] Hold the **Bellows** + right-click → the mannequin **swells and locks** (like a block); let go early and it shrinks back.
- [ ] **Tab** opens the **paint panel**: colour wheel, 16 dye swatches, eraser, and the Clothes / Mirror / Brush / Limbs options — each showing its letter shortcut.
- [ ] Pick a colour from the panel and **left-click** the mannequin → it paints, **with no dye in your hotbar**.
- [ ] **R** moves the limbs **apart**; the **inner arm**, **inner leg**, **leg top** and **body top/bottom** faces are all paintable once separated, and unreachable before.
- [ ] **V** (or the panel) toggles **Clothes on/off** — off hides them and your clicks land on the body; on shows them and your clicks land on them. Never the hidden one.
- [ ] A **brand-new skin** opens with clothes **off**; a skin imported from Minecraft **with a hat/jacket** opens with clothes **on**.
- [ ] Fly around it: **hold a dye + left-click** still paints; the panel **eraser** (or an empty hand) rubs out.
- [ ] **G** eyedropper grabs an on-skin colour; **M** mirror paints both sides; **Z**/**X** undo and redo.
- [ ] **P** pins → the skin is **saved + worn** and the mannequin deflates.
- [ ] Painting your **worn** skin and pinning updates your **avatar live**.
- [ ] **"Your look → Edit ✎"** on a skin drops you into the Workshop with that skin on the mannequin.

**Wardrobe ops**
- [ ] **Wear** a different skin → the **✓** moves and the avatar/first-person hand update.
- [ ] **Duplicate** → an independent copy; editing the copy leaves the original unchanged.
- [ ] **Rename** with Enter **and** with **✓**; an empty name is ignored.
- [ ] **Delete** a non-worn, non-last skin → it's gone, its thumbnail too.
- [ ] **Delete** on the **worn** skin is **disabled** (or shows *"Wear another skin first"*).
- [ ] **Delete** is disabled when only **one** skin remains.

**Export**
- [ ] Per-entry **"Export ⬇"** downloads a `.png` and shows the 3-step "wear it in real Minecraft" help; **Got it!** / `Esc` dismisses it.
- [ ] The downloaded PNG is a real **64×64** skin (opens in any image viewer; loads in the Minecraft launcher).
- [ ] Step 3 of the help names the skin's **own** arm shape — *choose Classic* for a Classic skin, *choose Slim / Alex* for a Slim one.

**Classic / Slim arms**
- [ ] **Your look → Arms** shows **Classic** selected on a new skin; clicking **Slim** narrows the arms on the 3D thumbnail.
- [ ] Doing that on the **worn** skin reshapes your **third-person body** and your **first-person hand** immediately.
- [ ] The setting **survives a restart** (desktop) / **a tab refresh** (web).
- [ ] **Duplicate** on a Slim skin produces a Slim copy.
- [ ] In the Workshop, **Tab → Arms → slim** reshapes the mannequin live; the **texel grid** on the arm front shows **3 columns**, and the yellow hover box still lands under the crosshair.
- [ ] **Pin** saves the Arms choice as well as the paint.

**Import from Minecraft**
- [ ] The dashed **"Bring in a Minecraft skin"** card opens the username box.
- [ ] A **real Java username** → that player's skin arrives as a new **"from Minecraft"** entry and is worn.
- [ ] An **Alex (slim) player's** username → the entry arrives with **Arms: Slim** and the toast says *"Slim-arm skin — arms set to Slim"*.
- [ ] A **made-up username** → friendly *"couldn't find anyone by that name"*, nothing else changes.
- [ ] With **no internet** → friendly *"can't reach Minecraft… try again"*, nothing else changes; the game keeps working.
- [ ] **Refresh** on an imported entry re-pulls the current skin.

**Persistence (the important one)**
- [ ] Make several changes (paint + pin a skin, a rename, an import, wear a different one),
      then **refresh the browser tab**, reopen **Your look** → **everything is still there**,
      with the same skin worn (imported entries keep their **"from Minecraft"** tag).

**Thumbnails**
- [ ] Every skin shows as a recognisable little **3D character**; after you paint + pin one, its
      thumbnail reflects the new look.
