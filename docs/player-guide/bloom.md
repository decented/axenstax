# How Places Come Alive (Bloom)

Every place in the world has a life of its own. Plants, trees, water and animals make a place feel alive. Bare, stripped ground makes it feel grey and still. Friends being there, a shared meal and things built together lift it too.

This is called **bloom**. It belongs to the **place**, never to a player. There is no bar to watch, no score and no number. You read it with your eyes and ears: colour, sound and creatures.

## What you see and hear

- **Colour.** A grey place loses its colour. The grass, the leaves and even the sky and the fog turn cool and pale. A lively place looks rich and a little warm. Most places sit somewhere in between, in normal colour.
- **Swaying plants.** Grass, flowers and crops sway gently where the place is alive enough. In a grey place they stand still.
- **Birdsong and crickets.** Birds sing by day and crickets sing at night. A grey place is quiet. So is dusk, and so is rain. If you hear birds, you are standing in a place that is doing well.
- **Pollen and fireflies.** In a lively place you may see slow drifting pollen by day and blinking fireflies at night. If you turn particles down in Settings you will see fewer of them, or none.
- **Villagers.** Villagers show how their place is doing. Now and then a happy puff or a little burst of confetti appears over a villager in a thriving place or during a Feast, and a grey puff appears where the place is grey and still.

## The four words

You never see a number, but in a Trial that measures a place you will see four words:

| Words | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Grey and still** | Nothing much lives here yet. |
| **Something's stirring** | The first signs of life. |
| **Waking up** | Getting there. |
| **Alive** | The place is thriving. |

You meet these words in the **[Grey Quarry](grey-quarry.md)**. Out in your own world, colour and sound do the talking.

## What counts as a place

The world is split into squares, each **16 blocks by 16 blocks** and as tall as the world. Each square has its own bloom. A wide grassy valley is made of many places, and one of them can be grey while its neighbours are fine.

Places near you are the ones the game keeps up to date. Bloom is worked out on your own screen, so two players looking at the same edge may see slightly different shades.

## What lifts a place

**Life**

- Plants. Grass, tall grass, flowers, crops, saplings, leaves and logs all count, and so does water.
- **Variety.** Different kinds count more than lots of the same kind. Three kinds of tree, a couple of crops, some flowers and a pond will beat a field of grass.
- **Animals.** Each different kind of ordinary animal standing in the place counts once. Villagers do not count. They are people, not wildlife.

A place does not need to be covered in plants. A fair patch of green goes a long way.

**People together**

- Two or more players in the same place for a while.
- A **[Feast](the-feast.md)**.
- **[Commons](commons.md)** ground.
- Things **built by more than one person**. See **[Who Built It](who-built-it.md)**.
- A place that is **lived in** over many days: people come back to it and sleep in a bed there. It needs some building to count as a place rather than a field someone walked across.
- A well-stocked **[Commons Shelf](commons-shelf.md)**.
- Ticking a job off a **[Site Board](site-board.md)** gives a small cheer.

Being alone is never a penalty. It just adds nothing.

## What holds a place back

- **Bare, stripped ground.** Stone, gravel and cobble with nothing growing.
- **One crop for miles.** A huge field of a single crop holds bloom back, and it can fall to **[blight](blight.md)**.
- **Walls everywhere.** A place that is almost all building and no open ground.
- **A looted shelf.** A Commons Shelf that was emptied dims its place a little until it is stocked again.

## What does not count against a place

- **Natural ground.** Desert, beach, mountain, snow and sea are their own normal. A rocky cliff, a cave mouth or a ravine that was always there is not grey.
- **Your build.** A house, a first mine or one chopped tree leave a place in normal colour. Built blocks are simply left out of the sum.
- **Roads.** Grass worn into a path by many feet is a sign people use the place. It is not damage.

## Pace

A place drifts down slowly, over tens of minutes, and comes back up faster. Trees are the slow part, because saplings grow by chance.

In **Creative** mode places stay vivid. Bloom is shown, but nothing there turns grey.

## Where it runs

Bloom works in single-player, on a LAN, online and in the web taster. It is only ever about colour, sound and life. It never changes how hard the game is, what drops, or what you can do.

Grown-ups can see world-wide totals in the **[Tally](the-tally.md)**. Those are about the whole world, never about a person.
