For parents.

It's an open-source block sandbox where your child owns what they build — not a Bitcoin game with a child bolted on. Here are the straight answers to the questions that matter. No spin.

The short version: it's free — no ads, no loot boxes, no paywall, and there is no money in the game. Nothing in it can be turned into Bitcoin or any other money.

What's the catch?

There isn't a hidden one. No ads, no loot boxes, no paywall. It's open source, and you can even run the whole thing yourself on your own machine. We're backed by people who choose to support the project, not by selling your child's attention.

What's the sign-in?

In the browser there is no sign-in at all — no account, no cookies that identify anyone, and saves stay on the device. On the desktop app, players can sign in with Signet — a portable identity you own, with no passwords and no email harvesting. Signet lets a parent hold the account and run a child's persona under it. Nothing to leak, nothing we can sell.

Is my child exposed online?

By default, no. Identities are private unless someone deliberately chooses to go public — there's no accidental public profile, and a child shows up as a handle they choose, not an account name we hold. Going public is always an explicit, opt-in choice.

There is no public list of worlds and no matchmaking with strangers. To play with someone else, one of you hosts a world at home and lets the other in. Anyone on the same Wi-Fi can see that hosted world and, once signed in, join it. A host can also share a room code so a friend elsewhere can join: anyone who is signed in and has the code can join, so share a code only with people you know. Online play by contact is limited to people in the host's contacts, or someone the host has sent an invite link to. A link lets in the first person who uses it, for that world only, and they can stay until the host stops hosting or blocks them. Players connected directly can see each other's IP addresses. In-game text chat exists only inside those hosted worlds on the desktop app, between signed-in players; the browser version has no chat, no sign-in and no multiplayer.

Is the hash part gambling?

No. There is no stake, no bet and no spin. Deep underground, a real calculation (a hash) decides where the rarest gem, the Satori, hides. It is a gem you keep and use in the game, never sellable and never worth any money. It is there to show how proof-of-work really works.

Is there real money in the game?

No. Nothing in the game can be turned into Bitcoin or any other money, and there is no wallet. A server's owner can offer their own optional Bitcoin layer on their own server; it is off by default and this project does not run one.

Still want to dig in?

It's open source — you (or someone you trust) can read exactly how it works.
